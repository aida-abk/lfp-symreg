── KEY FINDINGS ────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. t=0.1 vs t=100: utilisation differs by only 5.3%. These two thresholds produce near-identical models. t=100 provides no sparsification benefit and can be dropped from future sweeps.

3. At t=10000, degree 3 is most sparse (62.9% utilisation) while higher degrees remain denser. This suggests polynomial features of degree ≥ 2 have large coefficient magnitudes that resist thresholding.
